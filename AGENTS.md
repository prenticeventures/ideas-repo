# Working agreements

## Sync before starting work

- At the start of work in this repository, check the current branch, working tree, and GitHub remote for updates.
- If the working tree is clean and the current branch has an upstream, pull with `git pull --ff-only` before making edits.
- Preserve local changes and commits. If local changes, diverged history, or a missing upstream prevent a safe pull, explain the situation before proceeding; do not automatically stash, reset, rebase, or switch branches.
- Report authentication, network, or permission failures promptly, including what is needed to restore access.
- This is a start-of-work rule, not a request for scheduled background syncing.

## Payer scope for healthcare fraud research

- Cover Medicare (including Medicare Advantage and Part D), state Medicaid (including managed care and CHIP arrangements), private insurers and employer-funded plans, and other relevant federal health programs. Do not treat the Medicare experiment as the exclusive business scope.
- Separate reporting channels, enforceable/statutory recovery routes, discretionary cash rewards, noncash recognition and negotiated service contracts. Verify the actual funding source and payer product; the administrator brand alone is insufficient.

## Standalone research deliverables

- Write research reports for a reader seeing the findings for the first time. Integrate the full scope into one coherent report. Do not organize the report around draft history, revisions or what changed.
- Present program rules, payer coverage, data sources and case evidence before the detailed business assessment. Keep editorial change notes out of the research narrative.

## Healthcare research audience and data access

- Write the healthcare fraud report as readable business research for an experienced healthcare expert. Use complete sentences, correct sentence capitalization and plain language. Keep detailed legal interpretation in supporting references while retaining material eligibility and payment conditions in the main report.
- Consider government data available through applications, public-records requests and approved researcher access, as well as free downloads. Assess health information exchange access where relevant. The user is willing to consider a potentially valuable data purchase costing less than $10,000. This is a research budget ceiling, not authorization to purchase, submit applications or contact data holders.
- Focus the current data expansion on government sources. Do not assume paid commercial datasets, subscriptions, employees or new services are needed. Treat the user's healthcare expertise as an analytical strength, not as automatic access permission.
- Keep technical website error codes in machine-readable access logs. In business prose, state that a source could not be accessed, identify the missing evidence and explain the practical limit. Try an ordinary browser when automated retrieval fails.
- Make each reward route easy to scan for who may file, lawyer requirements, major filing/eligibility conditions and potential payment. Include sourced successful payment examples where available and explicitly identify routes without a verified example. Distinguish announced or awarded shares from documented payments without repetitive legal disclaimers.
- Follow the user's communication preferences, including no em dashes, en dashes or semicolons in normal prose.
