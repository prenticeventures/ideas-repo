# Payer coverage: Medicare, Medicaid and private insurance

Research date: October 25, 2026. This review covers Medicare, state Medicaid, commercial insurance, employer-funded plans and other federal health programs. It distinguishes who funds care from who administers claims. Coverage consists of national reward-program screening, eight insurer/product reporting-page checks and selected state data sources. It does not certify every insurance product or every state's current law and data-release policy.

## 1. Who pays determines the route

| Coverage | Funding/administrator distinction | Possible paid route, not automatic eligibility | Public-data position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original Medicare Part A | Federal hospital and institutional coverage | Federal FCA recovery, WHIP for qualifying criminal information, or a CMS small reward when all conditions fit | Hospital-by-diagnosis and procedure summaries exist. A FY2024 inpatient API sample returned a row. Patient charts are not included. |
| Original Medicare Part B | Federal physician and outpatient coverage | Federal FCA, WHIP, or CMS incentive reward as applicable | Physician and hospital outpatient summaries exist. The 2021 to 2023 physician experiment and CY2024 outpatient API test both worked. Institutional and professional claims are separate payment components. |
| Medicare Advantage / Part C | Medicare-financed coverage administered by private plans | Federal false-claim theories can involve risk-adjustment/other federal payments. WHIP/CWA depend on their own scope, originality and outcome rules | Public summaries exist, but the physician fee-for-service experiment does not cover all Advantage encounters or risk-adjustment records. Detailed encounter research files require authorized access. |
| Medicare Part D | Federal prescription benefit administered by private plans, including standalone and Advantage drug plans | Federal FCA/WHIP/CWA if facts and requirements fit. No universal pharmacy-tip percentage | Provider-and-drug summaries are public. CY2024 API sample worked. Total drug cost is not simply the government's net loss and does not prove a prescription was false. |
| State Medicaid fee-for-service | Joint federal and state program. The state pays enrolled providers directly | Federal or state FCA where applicable, WHIP, and specific state informant laws, not just state hotlines | National HHS provider/month summaries were tested. State directories, aggregate rates, and reports add context. The inspected national file has no state payer field. |
| State Medicaid managed care and CHIP arrangements | Private plans administer publicly financed benefits. CHIP coverage/payment arrangements vary | Federal/state false-claims or specific reward routes require tracing the government funding and actual false claim. The brand alone does not establish a private-insurance-only case | National file documentation includes Medicaid managed-care encounters/CHIP, but paid-amount meaning, completeness and state attribution require validation. Capitation and provider service payments are different transactions. |
| Commercial individual, employer fully insured, and exchange plans | Insurance company bears claims risk. Exchange subsidies can introduce separate public-funding facts | State insurance-fraud qui tam statutes where applicable. DOJ criminal rewards under their separate terms. Ordinary insurer intake pages reviewed do not advertise cash bounties | Public negotiated rates and selected out-of-network payment disclosures exist. These are not a comprehensive claims ledger or clinical evidence. |
| Private employer self-funded plans | Employer/plan bears risk. A familiar insurer may only administer claims | Do not assume insurance-fraud statute, federal FCA, or a payment share applies. ERISA, plan funding, possible criminal offenses and state-law applicability require fact-specific review | Rate disclosures and some all-payer aggregates may help. Detailed employer-plan claims are not freely available just because a third-party administrator is named. |
| TRICARE | Federal program with private contractors | A qualifying federal false-claims/criminal reward route may apply. Contractor hotline does not itself promise a bounty | Official contractor/program reporting routes verified. No free equivalent of the physician PUF for all TRICARE claims verified in this check. |
| VA / VA-paid community care | Federal care and procurement/payment arrangements | Potential federal false-claims/criminal route where facts fit. VA OIG intake itself makes no advertised award promise | VA OIG reporting page verified. No open provider-by-procedure claims ledger verified in this review. |
| FEHB / PSHB | Government employee/postal health programs with participating carriers. Not ordinary commercial coverage merely because carrier is private | Potential federal case/reward route requires claim/funding analysis. An OIG tip is not automatically payable | OPM's current carrier materials establish the separate programs. Direct OPM OIG retrieval was blocked, so its live intake details were not verified in this review. |

Federal/state award amounts, seals, disclosures, exclusions and payment triggers remain in [programs.md](programs.md). This table does not create new award entitlements.

Primary supporting sources: [CMS Medicare encounter methods, 2026](https://download.cms.gov/encounter_data/medicare%20advantage%20encounter%20data%20analytical%20methods%20June%202026%20Final.pdf), [CMS public-data catalog](https://data.cms.gov/data.json), [HHS Medicaid source](https://opendata.hhs.gov/datasets/medicaid-provider-spending/), [DOJ Independent Health resolution](https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdny/pr/medicare-advantage-provider-independent-health-pay-98m-settle-false-claims-act-suit), [Cigna explanation of self-funded responsibility](https://www.cigna.com/employers/cost-control/funding-solutions), [TRICARE reporting](https://tricare.mil/ReportFraud), [VA OIG reporting](https://www.vaoig.gov/hotline/online-forms), [OPM carrier materials](https://piv.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/carriers/fehb/).

## 3. State data and access

- California Medi-Cal: official DHCS material identifies a publicly accessible enrolled FFS provider directory, with directory updates at least quarterly. That supports enrollment/identity checks, not a claim that the provider rendered or billed particular services. Its comparative payment-rate page describes code-level rates, claims and beneficiary counts for selected services. The linked data document could not be opened in this check, so it is not marked as a tested download. [Directory requirements](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/providers-partners/provider-directory-requirements-for-medi-cal-fee-for-service-providers/). [comparative rates](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/medi-cal-fee-for-service-comparative-payment-rate-analysis/).
- California all-payer database: HCAI publishes aggregate reports, while more detailed claims/encounters require an application and approved access, with fees and use restrictions. Current documentation describes 2018 to 2024 requestable files. Calling this an all-payer database does not make detailed insurer claims freely downloadable or suitable for commercial bounty investigations. No application, payment or access request was made. [HCAI access rules](https://hcai.ca.gov/data/request-data/hpd-data-access-and-release/).
- New York: The official Health Data NY portal lists Medicaid provider enrollment and all-payer public-use products. We verified the portal, not a provider-billing extract. We could not access the Medicaid individual-record-release page, so its current authorization instructions remain unverified. Public access to patient claims is not inferred. [Official portal](https://www.health.data.ny.gov/).
- Florida: The state dashboard listing appeared in search, but we could not access the dashboard page directly. It is a source candidate, not a tested dataset. We verified the Medicaid reward statute against the official statute. [Dashboard candidate](https://ahca.myflorida.com/medicaid/agency-dashboards.html). [reward statute](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/409.9203).
- National state-program coverage includes 50 states plus D.C. The state CSV distinguishes specific reward laws, FCA routes, OIG status, and unknowns. It is not a complete current legal verification or dataset inspection for every state.

## 4. Public private-insurance data

CMS confirms public machine-readable disclosures of in-network negotiated rates and selected out-of-network allowed amounts/historical billed charges. These can help benchmark prices or relationships. They are not universal patient-level claims, medical necessity evidence, or proof of actual utilization for every listed contract rate. No large insurer file was downloaded in this review. [CMS public-data explanation](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/healthplan-price-transparency/overview/public-data).

CMS's October 2026 final-rule fact sheet describes expanded contextual information and future applicability dates. Those future taxonomy/utilization disclosures must not be treated as a dataset already available and tested on October 25, 2026. Publication, effectiveness, required compliance and actual files are separate milestones. No business conclusion here assumes those future files arrive on schedule or include the exact fields needed. [CMS 9882-F fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/transparency-coverage-final-rules-cms-9882-f).

## 5. Implications for evidence and payment

Medicare, Medicaid and commercial insurance offer different combinations of available data and possible payment routes. The Medicare experiment demonstrates one accessible screening method. State Medicaid has separate statutory rewards and data-quality questions. Commercial insurer reporting pages sampled do not advertise cash bounties. A lead needs original, corroborated evidence and a qualifying payment route regardless of payer.

Access limits: We could not access the New York record-release or Florida dashboard pages, so their current instructions and data details remain unverified. We also could not access the OPM OIG page, leaving its current intake details unverified. One Wellpoint manual could not be retrieved and its linked reporting site could not be opened, so we used an accessible official Anthem document as a narrower fallback. Two CMS sample queries initially could not be retrieved, but a later retry succeeded. The successful inpatient, outpatient, and Part D samples, along with all attempts, are preserved in [payer-access.json](payer-access.json). No user login, credentials, or paid access was required for the successful checks. The blocked official pages need to be accessible or supplied before relying on their detailed current procedures. We have not treated those details as verified.
