# Publicly documented whistleblower shares by route

Research date: October 25, 2026. This review looks for official government or court records that identify a relator's share, not just a large settlement. A relator is the private person or company that brings a case on behalf of a government under a qui tam law. A stated entitlement, settlement term, or court allocation is not by itself proof that the recipient's bank received the money.

## What is missing for an outside public-data analyst

- No primary source reviewed says Lincoln Analytics used only public data. DOJ names the relator and the share, but the settlement does not disclose its lead-generation method or evidence package.
- Integra's Symphony settlement confirms a $45,000 conditional share. It does not disclose the analysis or evidence behind the case. Integra's two earlier hospital analytics cases did not produce rewards in the court records reviewed. The Fifth Circuit affirmed dismissal of its Baylor Scott & White complaint in 2020. The Ninth Circuit directed dismissal of its Providence case in 2021. Those rulings show that statistical comparisons alone did not make those complaints sufficiently specific or rule out lawful coding explanations. [Fifth Circuit, Integra v. Baylor](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/unpub/19/19-50818.0.pdf) · [Ninth Circuit, Integra v. Providence](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/memoranda/2021/03/31/19-56367.pdf)
- Hunter Laboratories is a useful comparison-based public-funds example, but the record describes a competing lab and pricing observations. It does not establish use of public data or that a solo analyst could reproduce its information advantage.
- The Medi-Cal informer-reward statute is a separate route from a qui tam lawsuit. It allows DOJ to consider an award after recovery tied to a Medi-Cal provider's criminal conviction, caps the award at 10% of restitution or $1,000, whichever is lower, and excludes government employees and contractors acting on information found through their duties. No official record reviewed here documents an actual award under that provision. [California Welfare and Institutions Code section 14107.12](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=WIC&sectionNum=14107.12.)
- The Illinois ICFPA permits qui tam cases by interested persons and provides for a share of proceeds. The Safelite settlement is a completed example under the Act, but its public agreement leaves the relator allocation in a separate agreement. No official public record reviewed here quantifies an Illinois ICFPA whistleblower's paid share. [Illinois ICFPA](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=2039&ActName=Insurance+Claims+Fraud+Prevention+Act.&ChapAct=740%EF%BF%BDILCS%EF%BF%BD92%2F&ChapterID=57&ChapterName=CIVIL+LIABILITIES)
- None of the reviewed settlement agreements, announcements, or court opinions included a bank receipt, wire confirmation, or other direct proof that a relator personally received the stated amount. This memo therefore reports shares as announced, awarded, allocated, or contractually payable, according to the record, and does not call them verified cash receipts.

## Reading the amounts correctly

A settlement amount is what a defendant agreed to pay to resolve specified claims. It can include government restitution, civil penalties, state and federal shares, fees, costs, and relator awards. A settlement total is not automatically the amount of claims billed, the amount paid on those claims, or a final finding of fraud. In the examples above, only the primary record's specific share term or allocation is counted as the relator's potential award. Where the record says “will receive” or makes payment conditional, the amount is not treated as cash already received.
