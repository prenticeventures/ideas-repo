# What outside-data healthcare FCA cases show

Research date: 25 October 2026. This review asks whether public healthcare data can lead to a payment for an outside analyst. It summarizes court and government records. It is not legal advice or a forecast of success.

## Short answer

Some data analytics firms have received DOJ-announced shares of settlements. The public records do not show that these firms succeeded using public data alone. In two hospital cases, Integra Med Analytics relied mainly on statistical comparisons of Medicare claims. Courts dismissed both cases at the pleading stage because the statistics did not sufficiently distinguish fraud from lawful explanations. Those dismissals did not decide that no fraud occurred.

A separate case against a vascular practice and physician resulted in an announced settlement share of about $976,000 for Lincoln Analytics. The DOJ release does not describe the firm's methods or data sources. Another Integra case led to an announced $45,000 share on a $300,000 settlement based on the defendants' ability to pay. The releases say the relators will receive these shares. No bank receipts were independently verified here.

## Cases and what each one shows

| Case and date | What the records say about the relator | Outcome and any disclosed share | Practical meaning for an outside analyst |
|---|---|---|---|
| Integra Med Analytics v. Baylor Scott & White, Fifth Circuit, 28 May 2020 | Integra was an analytics firm, not an identified Baylor employee. It analyzed CMS inpatient claims from 2011 to 2017 and compared the use of high-payment major complication or comorbidity codes, called MCC codes. It also relied on statements from a former Baylor coder. | The Fifth Circuit affirmed dismissal of Integra's allegation that Baylor submitted $61.8 million in false claims. The court found the complaint did not plead a sufficiently specific, plausible fraud claim. The Supreme Court denied review on 7 December 2020. The opinions report no recovery or relator share. | Statistics plus a former employee's statements still did not make the allegations detailed enough to proceed. The dismissal was about the complaint, not a trial finding that Baylor did nothing wrong. |
| Integra Med Analytics v. Providence Health & Services, Ninth Circuit, 31 March 2021 | Integra's complaint relied mainly on statistical analysis of Medicare claims. The Ninth Circuit described the firm as lacking the usual insider's firsthand knowledge. A district court had allowed the main claim to proceed, then the defendants appealed before trial. | The Ninth Circuit reversed and ordered the main claim dismissed. It said higher rates of payment-increasing codes could have a lawful explanation: Providence and its consultant may have coded claims more effectively. The appellate opinion reports no settlement or relator share. No later Providence payment was found in the court or DOJ records reviewed for this memo. | A comparison that shows higher billing does not, by itself, explain why the claims were false. It must address plausible lawful reasons for the difference. |
| Lincoln Analytics and the Serrano vascular case, DOJ announcement, 6 May 2026 | DOJ identifies Lincoln Analytics as the qui tam relator. Qui tam is a lawsuit brought by a private party on behalf of the government under the False Claims Act, or FCA. DOJ's release and settlement agreement describe the allegations and share, but not Lincoln's methods or data sources. A secondary law firm summary describes public claims analysis and interviews, including a former employee. That account is not confirmed by the DOJ settlement record. | Serrano Kidney & Vascular Access Center and Dr. Feliciano Serrano agreed to pay more than $6.73 million to resolve allegations about unnecessary vascular procedures. DOJ says Lincoln will receive about $976,000 from the federal recovery. The settlement agreement sets Lincoln's share at 15% of federal settlement payments received. DOJ says there has been no decision on liability. | This is a positive payment example for an analytics company, but the primary records do not show that the case came from public data alone. The announced share is tied to federal payments as received, not proof that Lincoln had already collected the full amount. |
| Integra Med Analytics and the Symphony skilled-nursing case, DOJ announcement, 8 May 2026 | DOJ names Integra Med Analytics as the qui tam relator. The public announcement does not say how it found the case or whether it used insider or nonpublic information. | Three Illinois skilled-nursing facilities agreed to pay $300,000 to resolve allegations about unnecessary therapy. The amount was based on their ability to pay. DOJ says Integra will receive $45,000. The agreement resolved allegations, with no finding of liability stated in the release. | This is another announced share for an analytics firm. The settlement is much smaller and the public record does not reveal the firm's evidence or methods. |
| Teresa Ross and Independent Health, DOJ announcement, 20 December 2024 | Ross was a former employee of Group Health Cooperative, a different health plan that is now part of Kaiser. She was not an outside analytics firm or an Independent Health employee. DOJ does not describe her as a data analyst. Her case concerned DxID's reviews of medical records and diagnosis submissions. Ross also alleged that her own employer used DxID. | Independent Health agreed to guaranteed payments of $34.5 million and up to $63.5 million more, depending on its ability to pay. DxID had ceased operations. CEO Betsy Gaffney separately agreed to pay $2 million. DOJ says Ross will receive at least $8,212,500. DOJ states the settlement resolved allegations without a finding of liability. | The large announced share came from a relator with employment context and information about another health plan's use of the vendor, not from a public-data-only outside analyst. The government intervened in the case. |

## Valeant and Silbersher: separate court rulings and settlements

Zachary Silbersher is a public-information example, but the records reviewed do not show a paid FCA recovery for him. The Ninth Circuit reversed a dismissal based on the public-disclosure bar and sent the case back for further proceedings. That was a ruling about whether public materials disclosed substantially the same allegations. It was not a finding that Valeant violated the FCA or that Silbersher should receive money.

After remand, the district court dismissed the second amended complaint without prejudice in July 2025 and allowed another amendment. No FCA settlement or relator share was found in the court or DOJ sources reviewed here. Valeant also faced separate third-party-payer litigation and a class settlement. Those lawsuits had different plaintiffs and claims. Their settlement proceeds should not be described as Silbersher's FCA recovery.

## Claims that remain unconfirmed

- “CareCentrix $3 million data-analytics FCA case”: I did not find a matching DOJ or court record. The $3 million DOJ FCA settlement located was for Ocenture and Carelumina, with two marketer relators. A separate CareCentrix-related $6.3 million figure appears to concern a data-breach class settlement. Do not treat either as a CareCentrix analytics-relator award without a case citation.
- “Integra $100 million” or “Integra v. Providence 2024/2026”: I found no primary source tying those amounts or dates to the Providence case. Integra's Providence appellate case was decided in 2021 and directed dismissal. The separate 2026 Integra/Symphony settlement was $300,000.
- International Renal / DaVita: I did not confirm a relevant outside-data relator case in the primary records reviewed. A docket or DOJ source is needed before using it as an example.

## When the Serrano share may be paid

Lincoln filed its case on 30 May 2023. DOJ announced the settlement on 6 May 2026. The agreement schedules federal payments over time, including deadlines at 30, 180, and 364 days and quarterly payments over four years for the remaining $3.25 million. It also applies suspended funds. Lincoln receives 15% of federal payments when the government receives them, as soon as feasible afterward. The announced share of about $976,000 does not establish immediate payment of the full amount or a verified bank receipt. See [settlement agreement, recitals B and terms 1 to 3](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1439676/dl).
