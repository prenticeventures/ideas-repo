# Public agency reward examples

Research cutoff: October 25, 2026. This check looks for publicly documented payments or award decisions, not undisclosed payments. “No verified example found” means the reviewed agency material did not identify a public example. It does not mean no one has been paid.

Main use: public records show that IRS and SEC awards are paid at scale across all sectors, and CMS has a small historical total for its Medicare reward program. The public examples reviewed do not show that healthcare-specific IRS or SEC awards were paid.

## What the examples say about near-term economics

The only verified healthcare-program reward total in these examples is CMS’s historical report of $16,000 across 18 rewards, against approximately $3.5 million recovered. That is an agency-reported total from 2013. It does not establish current program volume or the likelihood, size, or timing of a new award.

A launch check was reported as announced, but no completed WHIP payment was independently verified. Its five-day operating history is too short to assess outcomes. CWA’s published number of tips and resulting investigations shows intake and enforcement activity, not award outcomes. The public IRS and SEC materials reviewed also do not let us connect a healthcare case to a particular whistleblower payment. These gaps make it unsafe to use an assumed reward as the base case for a business forecast.

## Limits of this check

I checked public primary materials from DOJ, CMS, IRS, and SEC. I did not find a public payment ledger, current CMS cumulative total, or named healthcare-specific IRS or SEC award example. Award confidentiality, delayed investigations, and the short WHIP operating period limit what public records can show. This is not a claim that payments did not occur.
