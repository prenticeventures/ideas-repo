# U.S. healthcare fraud detection and reward programs

Business research report | October 25, 2026

## Overview

There is a real opportunity to investigate healthcare fraud using data. There are also several ways to get paid for successful work. The unanswered business question is whether a solo operator can repeatedly turn a suspicious pattern into evidence that earns enough to cover unsuccessful investigations and long payment delays.

Public Medicare data are a useful starting point. Our experiment found a surgeon charged in a major atherectomy case among the national top twenty providers by selected procedure volume in each of three years. That shows an outsider could have seen the billing pattern. It does not show that the outsider could prove the procedures were unnecessary or earn a reward.

This report evaluates the opportunity for an experienced healthcare professional working alone with AI. It covers Medicare, Medicaid, private insurance and other federal health programs. The data strategy includes free downloads, government records available by request and controlled government research datasets. A data purchase costing less than $10,000 is an option if it would answer a specific question and the intended use is permitted. No purchase or outside request has been made.

### What the research supports

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can an outsider get paid? | Yes. Federal and state False Claims Act cases, DOJ reward programs and some state Medicaid informant laws provide payment routes. An ordinary fraud hotline usually does not. |
| Can analysis of public data qualify? | Sometimes. DOJ's new WHIP program expressly allows original public-data analysis. Lawsuit-based routes have additional requirements. Repeating a public allegation or producing an outlier list is not enough. |
| Is there useful accessible data? | Yes. Medicare billing, prescribing, provider identity, ownership, exclusions and other records support screening. Government applications and records requests may add useful detail, but access and permitted use must be checked. |
| What is the hardest step? | Showing what makes specific claims false, rather than merely unusual. Clinical records, actual service dates, referral arrangements or payer records may be needed. |
| Does healthcare expertise help? | Yes. It can improve code selection, clinical interpretation and the assessment of reasonable explanations. It does not by itself provide access to patient records or permission to reuse restricted data. |
| Is this ready to support dependable income? | No. A specialized investigation business is conditionally viable, but its cost per useful lead, payment rate and cash timing remain unproven. |

### How to read the findings

The report separates three stages. Screening finds something worth examining. Corroboration establishes facts that support a particular violation and tests reasonable explanations. Payment requires the outcome specified by the chosen program.

The case studies describe pending criminal charges, not findings of guilt. The financial examples distinguish claims submitted, amounts paid, recoveries and reporter awards. Those numbers are not interchangeable.

Detailed program terms, the 50-state plus D.C. index, data inventories and source records are linked throughout. The main report focuses on business decisions. The references preserve the legal and technical detail needed for further diligence.

## 1. How you could get paid

The main distinction is between a lawsuit that shares a recovery and a reward for useful information. Sending a fraud report does not automatically create either right.

### Federal programs and lawsuit routes

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Federal False Claims Act (FCA) | Individuals or qualifying entities. An insider job is not required. | Yes, to pursue the government's case. | Usually 15% to 25% of recovery if government takes over, or 25% to 30% if it declines and you succeed. | File a qualifying lawsuit and supply evidence. Recovery is required. Prior public disclosures, earlier cases and weak evidence can defeat it. Exceptions can reduce the share. |

### WHIP deserves a separate look

[Read the WHIP deep dive](WHIP-RESEARCH.md). It examines public-data eligibility, professional-source restrictions, the live intake format, funding and the first-award reporting. It also identifies unresolved wording on tips involving an existing government investigation. A named tip does not require a lawsuit or lawyer, but a qualifying charge and DOJ approval are still needed for payment.

### What matters most for your business

- FCA cases offer large potential shares, but require litigation and collection. The private plaintiff is called a relator. Attorney fees, case costs and who funds the work must be agreed. The statute does not supply upfront funding.
- WHIP is the clearest express fit for original public-data analysis. DOJ launched it on October 20, 2026, so there is no mature history for estimating success rates or payment delays. Charges are the normal milestone, not tip submission. [DOJ announcement and FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/fraud/criminal-tip-rewards-pilot-program-faqs).
- The corporate pilot pays after forfeiture, meaning the government has taken qualifying money or property connected to the crime. Alleged losses and large billed amounts are not its payment base.
- The Medicare reward's $1,000 ceiling is too small to support substantial investigation costs by itself.
- Selling analysis to an insurer or attorney would be a separate service business. No buyer, contract or willingness to pay was tested here.

### Can public-data analysis qualify?

Yes, under some conditions. WHIP expressly recognizes original analysis. For an FCA case, the question is whether your analysis reveals an important fact that was not already disclosed and supports specific false claims.

The public-disclosure rule can block a case that repeats substantially the same allegations already in certain public sources. An original-source exception can sometimes preserve it. These rules are separate from whether the underlying evidence is strong. A novel ranking can still fail to show fraud.

The practical test is straightforward: what does the analysis establish beyond “this provider bills more than peers,” and why is a lawful explanation less convincing? Courts have rejected cases where statistical differences remained consistent with proper coding. [Federal rule](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/3730), [court examples](research/outsider-precedents.md).

### Filing order, confidentiality and other programs

Before submitting a lead, select the route and check its disclosure and filing rules. Filing a sealed lawsuit, making an anonymous tip and sending a named agency report are different actions. Confidentiality is limited and can change during a case.

Do not add several program percentages in a revenue forecast. Overlap exclusions, earlier filings and deadlines can affect payment. The [program reference](research/programs.md) preserves the detailed rules, submission routes and sources. IRS and SEC programs are adjacent possibilities only if the evidence separately establishes tax or securities violations.

The source of the money matters more than the insurer's name. A company can administer commercial insurance, employer-funded benefits, Medicaid and Medicare Advantage. The same logo can therefore point to different data sources and payment routes.

| Original Medicare Part B | Federal physician and outpatient payments. | The strongest tested public-data route in this project. Physician and hospital outpatient files are separate payment components. |

## 3. Government data: downloads, requests and approved access

The data strategy should not stop at what can be downloaded today. There are three useful levels: open data for screening, government records or aggregates available by request, and controlled claims data available through an approved project. Each can add different evidence.

The existing [19-source inventory](research/datasets.csv) records fields, identifiers, years, access routes and test status. The [data audit](research/datasets.md) contains the detailed limitations. The [government access assessment](research/government-data-access.md) evaluates request-based routes, costs and permitted use. Documentation-verified access is not the same as an approved application or a tested delivery.

An additional [provider-pair data review](research/referral-data.md) verifies that the free CMS and NBER shared-patient files for 2009 through 2015 remain accessible. They contain provider pairs and aggregate counts without patient identifiers. Newer DocGraph files are listed separately under commercial and noncommercial licenses. The historical files do not overlap the 2021 through 2023 billing experiment, and patient sharing does not establish an actual referral or misconduct.

### The question to settle before ordering claims

Can the approved project produce provider-specific evidence that may be referred to enforcement or an attorney in connection with a potential reward?

CMS LDS terms require an approved research purpose, public dissemination, permission for external data links and protection of beneficiaries. They prohibit identifying or contacting beneficiaries. The terms do not clearly settle this project's proposed provider-specific referral and reward use. Paying for the files does not resolve that question. [CMS agreement](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/new-lds-dua-terms-conditions.pdf).

The 5% Carrier sample offers more detail than public summaries, but fewer patients. It samples beneficiaries rather than taking 5% of providers. It can miss smaller practices and rare events. We should not assume that it will reproduce full provider volume or resolve medical necessity. [Carrier file description](https://resdac.org/cms-data/files/carrier-ffs).

### Could a health information exchange help?

An HIE could contain clinical evidence that claims lack. The missing step is an authorized access relationship. No independent access route for this bounty business was established.

TEFCA supports defined purposes such as treatment, payment, healthcare operations and individual access. It does not offer an open investigator search. A legitimate arrangement with a payer or other authorized organization could change the assessment, subject to network agreements and applicable rules. No such arrangement, fee quote or access right exists in this project. Your healthcare experience helps interpret records but does not itself authorize queries. [Official TEFCA explanation](https://rce.sequoiaproject.org/tefca/how-it-works/).

CMS Blue Button is a different route: an approved app can access the records of a beneficiary who authorizes it. That is individual consent, not population-wide provider screening. [CMS documentation](https://bluebutton.cms.gov/api-documentation/).


### Choosing data for a specific fraud theory

A dataset is valuable when it changes what we can conclude. More rows alone do not accomplish that.

| Investigation question | Minimum useful additional data | What would still be missing |
|---|---|---|
| Is a provider an unusual procedure outlier? | Provider, code, period, setting, services and a defensible peer group. | Why the care was provided and whether claims were false. Public summaries already address much of this screen. |
| Are repeat procedures or combinations unusual? | Stable patient key, service dates, codes and relevant diagnoses. | Chart-level necessity, laterality detail where absent, actual treatment and intent. |
| Are services implausible because of licensing or exclusion dates? | Correct provider identity, service dates, historical status and the applicable payment rule. | Exceptions, attribution and whether the identified payment was actually prohibited. Annual totals may be too coarse. |
| Are referrals or ownership arrangements suspicious? | Reliable entity relationships, referral or ordering links, payment records and dates. | The actual agreement, services and intent. Ownership or industry payments alone do not establish misconduct. |
| Did a substance-treatment network bill for care that was not delivered? | Payer claims plus attendance, treatment records, staffing or other service evidence. | Public business and licensing records cannot establish what happened to each patient. |

Your healthcare experience is most useful in defining the right comparisons and testing these explanations. AI can accelerate extraction and reconciliation, but it cannot supply missing observations.

### Data-quality findings that affect the business

The national Medicaid file is not ready for an unqualified ranking by dollars. A small preserved sample contained a paid amount in the trillions and an identifier with no NPPES result. This could reflect source, formatting or definition problems. It is not a fraud finding, and the sample does not describe the quality of the entire file. The public CSV is roughly 11.55 GB, with a roughly 3.07 GB Parquet option listed. Validate amounts, identifiers, procedures and missingness before expanding analysis. [Source and files](https://opendata.hhs.gov/datasets/medicaid-provider-spending/).

Provider location also does not establish which state Medicaid program paid. That distinction matters when choosing a state reward route. Public Medicare files have different limits: annual publication delay, small-cell suppression and no patient linkage across procedure rows. Service units cannot be read as unique patients or operations. [CMS methods](https://data.cms.gov/sites/default/files/2025-04/MUP_PHY_RY25_202350312_Methodology_508.pdf).

### Price disclosures and other commercial-plan context

Public plan files contain negotiated rates and selected out-of-network allowed amounts. They can support price comparisons, but they are not a full paid-claims database. No large insurer price file was downloaded in this project. Future disclosure requirements in CMS's October 2026 rule are not treated as data already available. [Current public files](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/healthplan-price-transparency/overview/public-data), [new rule and applicability dates](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/transparency-coverage-final-rules-cms-9882-f).

The current priority is government data. This report does not propose a commercial data subscription or assume a commercial vendor is needed.

## 4. What the two saved fraud cases demonstrate

Both principal prosecutions were pending at the October 25, 2026 cut-off. The defendants are presumed innocent. The full [case studies](research/case-studies.md) and [source index](research/case-sources.csv) preserve the court identifiers, chronology and record details.

### Atherectomy: Harold Tabaie and Hany Malek

The indictment alleges that a Southern California clinic network billed Medicare for unnecessary vascular procedures, used patient inducements and submitted some claims under other physicians' names. It describes a scheme from January 2016 through August 2023. The defendants were charged in October 2026. The alleged totals are approximately $134,955,000 submitted and at least $54,817,000 paid. These are charging-document figures, not final losses or recoveries. [Indictment](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/media/1467741/dl?inline=).

DOJ expressly says data analytics identified Tabaie as a national atherectomy billing outlier. That makes this a strong example for testing public-data screening. It does not identify the government's exact dataset or full investigative method. [DOJ local announcement](https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/la-health-care-fraud-takedown-results-15-defendants-charged-including-six-medical).

An outsider could link Tabaie's identity to NPI 1346286663 and compare his procedure volume with peers in CMS's public files. Our experiment did that. The 2023 data were public by May 2025, before the indictment, although we did not establish when investigators first found the case or whether these exact data versions were available then.

The missing evidence is decisive. Public aggregates do not show imaging, indications, operative notes, actual rendering clinician, referral discussions or patient inducements. The allegation that claims used other doctors' names also means an individual-NPI screen can miss part of a network.

Business lesson: the initial screen is feasible and cheap. Building a case about unnecessary care requires a second source of evidence. No reviewed record documents a reporter reward in this case.

### Substance-treatment network: Nathan Young and related defendants

The principal indictment charges Nathan Young, David Young, Honey Phipps and Cody Phipps in a network involving dozens of facilities. It alleges patient brokering, inducements, false enrollment and billing for unnecessary, deficient or unprovided care from May 2020 through May 2026. It identifies private insurers including Anthem, Aetna, Cigna and UnitedHealthcare. The alleged totals are $1,619,276,243 submitted and at least $210,274,793 paid. [Public copy of indictment](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/usa-young-indictment.pdf).

Those figures are not a $1.6 billion recovery or a proven $210 million loss. A related guilty plea by Nicholas Moore concerns his own case and does not establish the guilt of the four principal defendants. The federal prosecution's origin is not identified in the reviewed sources. DOJ names investigating agencies, but that does not tell us who first supplied the lead.

Some concerns were public earlier. A Beverly Hills nuisance case dates to 2020, and Aetna brought a civil fraud action in 2023 with a later amended complaint. Reporting also described an ongoing federal investigation before the indictment. Those records could help map entities and allegations. They do not establish that public records caused the investigation or that repeating them would produce a new, payable tip. The [case reference](research/case-studies.md) identifies these records and their limits.

An outsider lacks a Medicare-style public claims ledger for these commercial insurers. Testing the core allegations would require evidence about enrollment, attendance, treatment, broker payments and insurer claims. A government all-payer database might improve utilization visibility if access and use were approved. It would not, by itself, reveal whether therapy actually occurred.

Business lesson: public records can reveal a network worth investigating, but the main evidence gap is wider than in the atherectomy screen. No reporter award was identified in the reviewed records.

## 5. What the Medicare experiment found

[Explore all 5,730 retrieved records in the interactive dashboard](experiment/provider-data-dashboard.html). Search providers, inspect original fields, compare billing volumes and export filtered results.

The experiment used free official data to ask a narrow question: would a simple billing-volume screen have surfaced the surgeon in the known atherectomy case?

Eighteen CMS API requests returned 5,730 rows for 2021, 2022 and 2023. The four primary codes were 37225, 37227, 37229 and 37231. Additional-vessel codes 37233 and 37235 were retrieved separately and excluded from primary totals. Comparisons held year, code definition and facility versus non-facility setting fixed. Same-specialty ranks use the specialty recorded in that year's CMS billing file.

| Year | Tabaie primary service units | National non-facility rank | Same-specialty rank | Estimated payment for selected primary codes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2021 | 495 | 12 of 695 | 2 of 271 | $3.79 million |
| 2022 | 598 | 7 of 711 | 1 of 274 | $3.88 million |
| 2023 | 429 | 14 of 695 | 1 of 283 | $2.67 million |

Payment estimates multiply service units by published average Medicare payment and sum the selected rows. They are not claim-level audits or estimates of fraud loss. Beneficiaries are not summed across codes because the same person can appear in several rows. Additional-vessel units are not additional operations.

The first download-and-analysis run took 4.52 seconds in this environment. An offline rerun took about one second and reproduced the results. That is computer runtime, not time spent researching or building a case. The code, fixed source versions, cached responses, hashes and instructions are included in the [experiment folder](experiment/README.md).

A desk review of the twenty highest-volume 2023 results considered specialty, small comparison groups, repeat-service ratios and possible referral concentration. No patient records were available to test clinical explanations. High volume can reflect specialization, severe disease, referral patterns, billing units or attribution. The review does not label the other providers as fraudulent.

### What the result establishes

A public-data screen can identify a meaningful billing outlier and can be reproduced at very low retrieval cost. Tabaie appeared in the national top twenty in all three years under the fixed method.

It does not estimate fraud-detection accuracy. We chose the case after enforcement made it known, which creates hindsight bias. The current snapshots may differ from earlier releases. A [CMS bulletin](https://www.cms.gov/training-education/medicare-learning-network/newsletter/2025-05-22-mlnc) confirms 2023 data were public by May 22, 2025, but not that these exact versions were public before the alleged scheme ended or before investigators began work. Full-year 2023 also includes months outside the alleged conduct window.

Reproducing an already-public prosecution does not create an original lead. The next test must examine a different uncertainty: whether additional records can support a specific violation in a new case.

### Adjacent tax and securities programs

### Cases that did not meet the evidence standard

Positive examples need to be read alongside failures. In Integra / Baylor, the Fifth Circuit upheld dismissal where statistical differences did not adequately separate fraud from lawful explanations. The record included a former coder, so an insider connection did not solve the problem by itself. An unpublished Integra / Providence decision also required dismissal where the analysis did not rule out lawful coding differences. [Baylor opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/unpub/19/19-50818.0.pdf), [Providence decision](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/memoranda/2021/03/31/19-56367.pdf).

Silbersher / Valeant shows that public-record analysis can raise a serious legal question without yielding an identified award. The appeals court reversed a public-disclosure ruling and sent the case back. Later proceedings did not produce a recovery found in this research. [Precedent review](research/outsider-precedents.md).

DOJ's FOCUS initiative explicitly addresses data-analysis plaintiffs and emphasizes reliable analysis, a relevant legal rule and corroboration. This supports the opportunity to do serious investigation work. It does not supply a solo founder's success rate. [DOJ memorandum](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1438871/dl).

## 7. Business feasibility and economics

### Assessment: conditionally viable as specialized investigation

The opportunity is credible enough to justify a small next test. It is not yet supported as dependable income from automated tips.

The strongest model combines healthcare judgment, careful data work and investigation. Public data find candidates. Requested records or approved claims access may answer narrower questions. The work earns value when it explains why particular conduct is inconsistent with a payment rule and survives reasonable clinical and operational explanations.

Your healthcare background is an advantage at that interpretation stage. The expanded data budget also creates options beyond public summaries. Neither is a reason to buy a broad dataset before checking whether it contains the missing evidence and permits the proposed use.

The operating sequence is screening, corroboration, program selection, evidence preparation, investigation or litigation, collection and payment. Costs begin early. Government priorities, litigation decisions and the defendant's ability to pay remain outside the founder's control.

### What smaller awards require to break even

Use an illustrative lead cost of 50 founder hours at $50 per hour plus $500 cash, totaling $3,000. The hourly amount values founder time. The cash allowance is a scenario, not an expense already incurred or a quote for legal services.

| Eventual gross award | Required share of $3,000 leads that must get paid |
|---|---:|
| $10,000 | 30% |
| $20,000 | 15% |
| $30,000 | 10% |

These are break-even requirements, not estimated success rates. They must cover unsuccessful leads as well as winners. Taxes and payment delays would make the economics less favorable.

At a $20,000 award, the required payment rate is 7.5% for a 20-hour lead costing $1,500, 15% for a 50-hour lead costing $3,000 and 27.5% for a 100-hour lead costing $5,500. Each assumes the same $50 hourly value and $500 cash allowance. A fast query does not imply a fast investigation.

### How a data purchase changes the calculation

A purchase should be justified by the missing fact it can reveal. For example, service dates might test an exclusion-period theory. More annual provider totals would largely repeat the existing screen.

Here is the effect of a hypothetical $9,000 dataset shared across investigations. Keep the earlier $3,000 base cost per lead and a $20,000 eventual award fixed.

| Leads sharing the purchase | Data cost allocated to each lead | Total cost per lead | Required payment rate to break even |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $9,000 | $12,000 | 60% |
| 5 | $1,800 | $4,800 | 24% |
| 10 | $900 | $3,900 | 19.5% |

The allocation only works if the approved use covers all those investigations and they can actually be completed. It is not a reason to assume ten viable leads. Data fees are paid before uncertain awards, so the business needs cash for both the purchase and unsuccessful work. Detailed scenarios are in [economics.csv](research/economics.csv).


### Larger recoveries and cash timing

A $5 million collected recovery at a 15% to 25% share would produce $750,000 to $1.25 million gross for the private plaintiff. If counsel took a hypothetical 40% of that award, $450,000 to $750,000 would remain before other expenses, tax and any partner allocation. That fee is an illustration, not a market quote or an agreed arrangement. Statutory fee awards and actual engagement terms could change the result.

Payment can take years. In the Serrano matter, Lincoln filed in May 2023 and DOJ announced settlement in May 2026, about 35 months later. The agreement includes initial payments, withheld funds and a four-year installment schedule for part of the remaining federal balance. The plaintiff receives 15% of each federal payment as the government collects it. The announced award is therefore not evidence of one immediate check. Actual receipt dates were not verified. [Settlement agreement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1439676/dl).

For illustration, $450,000 received after one, three or five years has a present value of about $409,091, $338,092 or $279,415 at a 10% annual discount rate. These are scenarios, not observed case-duration probabilities. A failed case produces no award. A small settlement or insolvent defendant can sharply reduce a nominally attractive share.

### What could make the work difficult to copy

A ranking dashboard is easy to reproduce. More valuable work connects the correct providers and entities, applies a specific reimbursement rule, reconstructs dates, adds independent evidence and explains the strongest lawful alternatives. This is where clinical expertise and narrowly chosen additional data can matter.

An attorney partnership could supply legal screening and litigation capacity. An authorized payer, patient or insider source could supply facts that open records lack. None of those arrangements has been established here. They solve different gaps and should not be assumed to be available or free. Reward sharing, information rights and conflicts would need review before an agreement.

## 8. What remains uncertain

The main business unknowns are practical:

1. Can a new investigation produce facts beyond unusual billing at a reasonable time cost?
2. Can government data be obtained with the needed fields, dates and provider identifiers under terms compatible with the actual intended use?
3. Would any requested dataset materially change the assessment of a lead, or would patient charts and other unavailable evidence still be decisive?
4. Is a proposed lead new to the authorities, and would it qualify for the selected program?
5. How often will a strong lead produce a payment, how much will it pay and when?
6. Would counsel accept the evidence and on what cost and funding terms?
7. Can the national Medicaid file's questionable values and identifiers be resolved before it is used for ranking?

Some source checks remain incomplete. New York's record-release page and Florida's dashboard could not be accessed. Louisiana's current reward rule was verified in the official code, while funding and payment history remain unknown. The OPM OIG website could not be accessed. A Wellpoint manual timed out and a reporting link failed, so a narrower accessible Anthem document was used instead.

Other access problems were resolved. The three relevant California statutes were read on the official website through the normal browser. Two CMS sample requests initially hit a rate limit and later succeeded. The [access log](research/access-log.json) and [payer access evidence](research/payer-access.json) retain these distinctions.

The complete current criminal dockets and a separate Young complaint affidavit were not obtained. Public status and the located indictments do not establish every later court event. No claims application, license-data approval, patient authorization, HIE enrollment or data purchase has been made. Where a data route is described as possible, it remains subject to the actual application and use terms.

## 9. The smallest useful next tests

The completed experiment tested whether public billing data could reproduce a known outlier. Repeating that screen with more providers would not answer the main business question. Two small tests would add different information.

### Test A: can public records add evidence beyond high volume?

Hypothesis: at least one of up to three existing California results can be supported by independent dated records that point to a specific payment-rule violation and address a reasonable lawful explanation.

Exact action: review up to three California providers already in the 2023 non-facility results, in the existing volume order, with at least 30 published same-specialty peers. Exclude the named historical target and already-public enforcement allegations. Use the correct licensing board, historical disciplinary and exclusion records, existing court documents and the rules applicable to the service dates. Prepare an internal one-page evidence-and-gaps summary for each.

Keep fixed: the existing snapshots, four primary codes, setting, rank order and selection criteria. Do not run a new national screen or lower the criteria to force a result.

Cost and measurement: maximum six founder hours and $0 new spend. Count how many summaries identify the actor, rule, relevant dates and a contradictory fact supported by at least two independently originating records. Record review time and remaining evidence gaps. Two copies of the same allegation count as one source.

Stop rule: stop after three providers or six hours. If every result remains only an aggregate billing difference, stop expanding that public-data-only approach. A missing result from one licensing board does not establish unlicensed practice. A positive result warrants further review, not a public allegation or automatic submission.

### Test B: can a government data request close a specific gap?

Hypothesis: a government data holder can supply or approve a narrowly defined dataset that answers an evidence gap the public aggregates cannot, at a total cash cost below $10,000.

Exact action: prepare one CMS LDS scope inquiry for a single-year Carrier study using the same four primary codes as the completed experiment. Disclose the solo commercial structure, possible provider-specific enforcement or attorney referrals and contingent reward. Ask about allowed use, sample size, provider identifiers, outside linkage and the computing environment. A draft is included in the [government data assessment](research/government-data-access.md).

Keep fixed: the procedure family, existing public screen and stated commercial purpose. Do not disguise a bounty investigation as unrelated academic research. Do not order files until both use permission and evidence value are clear.

Cost and measurement: cap preparation at two founder hours and $0 spend. Record whether there is a written answer covering eligibility, fields, permitted outputs and all required charges. If the first route fails, Maine is a separately assessed alternative, rather than an automatic second purchase.

Stop rule: stop that route if it does not permit the actual use, cannot supply the necessary fields, exceeds the budget, or leaves the provider sample too small to test the chosen question. If permission remains unclear, treat access as unresolved and do not purchase. Sending the inquiry would require a separate instruction because this research project excludes outside contact.


Both tests are proposals. This project has not contacted providers, patients, attorneys, agencies or data holders, submitted tips or requested records. No external action or purchase follows automatically from this report.

## 10. Research methods and supporting material

The research cut-off is October 25, 2026. The operator is an experienced healthcare professional working alone with AI and existing computing. Public downloads were used for the completed analysis. The broader assessment now includes government records requests and approved research access, with a potential data purchase below $10,000 if justified. No employees, commercial subscriptions or paid research services are assumed.

Sources prioritize official program terms, statutes, agency documentation, court opinions and actual data. The report identifies secondary statute hosts and incomplete access checks. No agency, insurer, attorney, provider or patient was contacted. No application, purchase, tip or lawsuit was submitted.

The Medicare experiment used fixed source versions and definitions, saved response hashes, filter and duplicate checks, independent arithmetic checks and an offline reproduction. The state index is a screening resource, and the insurer review covers the specified pages and products. Neither is an exhaustive certification of every current rule or private agreement.
