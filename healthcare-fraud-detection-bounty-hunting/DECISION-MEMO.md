# Healthcare fraud detection: executive summary

Business research | October 25, 2026

A specialized healthcare fraud investigation business is conditionally viable. Paying routes, useful data and successful analytics-company cases exist. Reliable income from automated tips has not been demonstrated.

Your healthcare expertise is useful for selecting procedures, assessing coding and clinical explanations, and deciding which evidence matters. The strongest opportunity is turning a billing pattern into a well-supported account of a specific violation.

## How you could get paid

| Route | Who can qualify? | Lawyer? | Potential payment and main gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Federal False Claims Act | Individuals or qualifying entities. Outsiders can bring cases. | Yes for litigation. | Usually 15% to 25% when government takes over, or 25% to 30% when it declines and you succeed. A qualifying case and recovery are required. |
| DOJ WHIP | Individuals, not companies. | No for named tips. Yes for anonymity. | Discretionary $10,000 to $30,000 per distinct qualifying tip leading to normally public criminal charges. Original public-data analysis can qualify. |
| Corporate whistleblower pilot | Eligible individuals. | No for named tips. Yes for anonymity. | Percentage of qualifying forfeiture. More than $1 million in net proceeds and program conditions are required. |
| State programs | Varies by law. | Litigation requires counsel in practice. Separate information rewards may not. | State recovery shares or capped informant rewards. Florida's separate Medicaid cap is the lesser of 25% recovered or $500,000, subject to funds. |
| California insurance-fraud action | Eligible interested persons. | Plan on counsel. | Generally 30% to 40% with specified government involvement, or 40% to 50% when authorities decline and you succeed. Conditions and allocation exceptions apply. |
| Ordinary insurer or agency report | Often anyone can report. | Usually no. | No general cash award established merely for reporting. |

See the [route-by-route report](REPORT.md) for sources, requirements and verified payment examples, and the [WHIP deep dive](WHIP-RESEARCH.md) for its new opportunity and unresolved rules. WHIP launched on October 20, 2026, so its track record is too short to estimate payment rates or delays.

## Which data are worth pursuing

- Start with free Medicare billing, prescribing, ownership, licensing and exclusion records. Several downloads and APIs worked, including the historical experiment.
- Include government data available by request. Existing aggregate reports, public-records requests and free bulk licensing files can add evidence without buying claims.
- Consider CMS limited data sets only after confirming intended use and analytical value. The listed annual 5% Carrier file is $1,700, but a beneficiary sample may be too small for a particular provider theory. Approval and security requirements apply.
- Maine offers a potentially affordable narrow claims request. Massachusetts is a provisional price-screen candidate. Neither has approved this project or supplied a quote.
- California HPD starts at $10,000 for one analyst and project, outside the stated less-than-$10,000 limit. Detailed Medi-Cal access has additional purpose restrictions. CMS Innovator research access is substantially over budget.
- No independent HIE access route was established. A legitimate authorized relationship could change that, but healthcare expertise alone does not grant access.

The [government data assessment](research/government-data-access.md) records prices, eligibility, fields and questions to settle before spending. Commercial data vendors are not the current priority.

## What the evidence shows

The Medicare experiment retrieved 5,730 rows through 18 free API requests. Tabaie ranked in the national top twenty for selected primary atherectomy volume in all three years. In 2022 he ranked 7th of 711 published non-facility providers and 1st of 274 within the recorded vascular-surgery group. This is a reproducible retrospective signal, not proof of unnecessary care or early discovery.

The atherectomy and substance-treatment cases show different evidence gaps. The former has a public Medicare screening analogue. The latter depends much more on commercial claims, treatment and enrollment records. Both principal prosecutions were pending, and no reporter award was identified in either.

DOJ announced about $976,000 for Lincoln Analytics and $45,000 for Integra in selected settlements. Those records establish recovery allocations, not public-data-only success or verified profit. Lincoln's agreement ties payment to government collections, including installments.

## Economics and next decision

At an illustrative $3,000 cost per researched lead, a $20,000 award requires 15% of leads to get paid just to break even before tax and delay. A hypothetical $9,000 data purchase spread over five leads raises that requirement to 24%. These are required rates, not estimated probabilities.

The next useful work is a bounded evidence test, not a larger outlier list. Review up to three existing California results for independent evidence of a specific rule violation, capped at six hours and $0 spend. Separately prepare a narrow government access inquiry to determine whether requested claims can answer the missing question under acceptable use terms and cost.
