The question we tested

Could free public Medicare data reproduce the high-volume billing signal in the already-known Tabaie atherectomy case?

How providers were selected

Procedure first. We pulled every published provider/code/setting row for six selected atherectomy codes in 2021, 2022 and 2023, across all states and specialties. We did not preselect suspicious NPIs.

What the original test showed

The known target ranked 12th, 7th and 14th by primary-code volume in the non-facility group. This demonstrates retrospective visibility, not discovery of a new fraud.

Exact cohort and what this test does not answer
  • Source: Original Medicare fee-for-service Part B professional billing summaries, not a patient-level claims file or a hospital-outpatient-only dataset.
  • Primary codes: 37225, 37227, 37229 and 37231. Additional-vessel codes: 37233 and 37235, kept separate in the original experiment.
  • All retrieved rows are U.S. records. Both facility and non-facility settings are included. CMS omits provider/code/setting cells involving ten or fewer beneficiaries.
  • The full retrieval contains 1,297 individual NPIs and 27 organization NPIs. Individual providers can include nonphysician practitioners. The original ranking excludes organizations and uses only the four primary codes.
  • The case and target identity were known in advance. This test cannot estimate fraud-detection accuracy, establish medical necessity, or show whether a new tip would earn a reward.
  • The charts below let you explore these saved data. Their selected-code and filtered ranks are separate from the fixed original experiment.

Each record is an annual provider × procedure × setting summary, not an individual claim. Original Medicare fee-for-service Part B only. CMS omits cells involving ten or fewer beneficiaries. Billing volume does not establish fraud.

How to interpret this comparison

Visible provider and setting records for the selected year.
National and specialty ranks
Original ranks use all published U.S. individuals in the same year and setting. State and specialty filters change the visible list, not those stored ranks. Ties share a rank.
Service units are not operations
Different codes and additional-vessel billing can represent parts of one treatment. Patient counts are not added across codes.
Clinical differences remain unknown
Referral concentration, disease severity and practice focus can explain billing differences. These records do not measure medical necessity.

Useful definitions

Primary codes
37225, 37227, 37229 and 37231. The experiment's selected lower-extremity atherectomy code family.
Additional-vessel codes
37233 and 37235. These units are kept separate rather than counted as extra operations.
Non-facility / facility
CMS setting categories O and F. Non-facility is an aggregate payment setting, not a precise clinic or location identifier.
Beneficiaries and payment estimates
Beneficiaries belong to an individual code and setting row. Estimated Medicare payment uses the published average times service units. Rounding can differ from an exact claim-level total.

Limits of this retrieval

These are provider summaries, not patient-level claims or clinical records. Small cells are suppressed. The files omit Medicare Advantage, Medicaid and private-insurance claims. Annual rows cannot isolate an individual charged service date or explain whether treatment was appropriate.

The 2023 physician data were public by May 2025, before the October 2026 indictment. These exact revised snapshots were not shown to be available before the alleged scheme ended or before the government began investigating.
