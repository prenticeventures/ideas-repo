# Cleaning, refurbishing, and certifying DME for healthcare

- Saved: 2026-10-24
- Category: public good corporations (Teera's words: "public good corporations or whatever"; category name tentative)
- Status: raw idea, no research yet

## Idea

A business that cleans, refurbishes, and certifies durable medical equipment (DME) for healthcare use.

## Workforce idea

Possibly employ formerly homeless or incarcerated people.

## Open questions

- What certification standards apply to refurbished DME, and who issues them?
- Which equipment categories make sense to start with?
- Who are the buyers: hospitals, clinics, insurers, patients?

## Adjacent example to research: MediCircle

- Added: 2026-10-25 (found via the Out of Pocket newsletter)
- Site: https://medicirclerx.com

MediCircle reclaims unused, unexpired medications and redispenses them to patients for free. Donors answer a few questions, receive a free Donation Kit, ship their meds back for free, and are notified when their medication is matched to a patient. The company claims over $72 million in medication value recertified through its patented process to date. It operates as a licensed pharmacy (Texas pharmacy license 34472, NPI 1114645074, based in Sugar Land, TX) and pitches health plan partnerships as a way to cut pharmacy spending.

Why it is adjacent to this idea: same core loop, reclaim, recertify, redistribute, but for medications instead of durable equipment. The DME version would swap pharmacy law for device cleaning, refurbishing, and certification standards, and would need its own answer to MediCircle's key questions: what the recertification process is, which regulations permit redistribution, and who pays (patients, health plans, donors, or grants).
